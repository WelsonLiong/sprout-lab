# SCES3274 Task 1 · Design rationale draft

Sprout Lab is an interactive website for Year 4 pupils learning photosynthesis. I unpacked curriculum standards 4.2.1–4.2.4 into three learning objectives: explain how green plants make food, identify conditions and products, and describe why photosynthesis matters to living things (Kementerian Pendidikan Malaysia, 2018). Pupils can revisit six short, clearly organised stops instead of following a timed quiz. This gives them time to test ideas, discuss observations and revise explanations.

The resource begins with a familiar question: where does a rooted plant get food? Pupils make a prediction before watching a visual leaf explanation. In the lab, they remove one condition and compare the model's response with their prediction. The creek puzzle distinguishes water absorbed by roots from food made in leaves. Nico's forest adventure connects sunlight, water, green leaves, animals and air through visible environmental changes. A final reflection asks pupils to say what they used to think and what they now understand.

These choices support Meaningful Learning Pedagogy by linking prior ideas to new evidence, encouraging active inquiry and making Science useful beyond a single screen. The teacher can prompt partner talk, question pupils about cause and effect, and use their explanations as formative evidence. The forest gives contextual feedback and allows retrying. Its certificate celebrates completion without presenting a mark or claiming an official curriculum performance level.

Age suitability guided the design. Instructions are brief, controls are labelled, and scientific terms appear beside relevant actions or diagrams. Illustrated scenes make the topic inviting while interactive diagrams carry the scientific meaning. Keyboard and touch controls, captions, optional read-aloud, reduced motion and a printable trail widen access. Progress is saved locally under an optional nickname, so pupils do not need accounts or personal details. Sources and asset credits remain visible, and the teacher should verify the resource before classroom use.

## Reference
