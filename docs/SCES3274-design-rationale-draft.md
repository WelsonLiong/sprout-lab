# SCES3274 Task 1 · Design rationale draft

Sprout Lab is an interactive website for Year 4 pupils learning photosynthesis. It addresses learning standards 4.2.1–4.2.4 of the Science Year 4 curriculum (Kementerian Pendidikan Malaysia, 2018). I unpacked these standards into learning goals: explain how green plants make food, identify the conditions and products involved, and describe why the process matters to living things. Pupils demonstrate success by predicting, testing a condition, explaining observations and revising an initial idea. Pupils may revisit stops freely.

The six stops can support a collaborative inquiry cycle. Pupils first consider where a rooted plant gets food, then explore a visual leaf explanation. In the lab, they remove one condition and compare the response with their prediction. A creek puzzle distinguishes water absorbed by roots from food made in leaves. Nico's forest adventure connects sunlight, water, leaves, animals and air through visible environmental changes. A final reflection invites pupils to explain what changed in their thinking.

This sequence applies Pedagogical Capacity for Deep Learning (PCDL) through engagement, enhancement and extension. The mystery engages prior knowledge; interactive models enhance understanding by making cause and effect visible; forest decisions extend learning to a connected ecosystem. It also follows the 3R framework: the puzzles provide rigor through evidence-based explanations, relevance through familiar plants and habitats, and relationship through partner talk. Teachers can ask pairs to justify predictions, compare results and improve explanations. Their responses provide formative evidence against the learning goals.

Short instructions, labelled controls and nearby scientific terms suit young learners. Keyboard and touch controls, captions, optional read-aloud, reduced motion and a printable trail widen access. Progress stays on the device under an optional nickname, without pupil accounts. The forest is unscored, and its certificate records completion rather than a curriculum performance level. The teacher should check the resource and its source credits before classroom use.
